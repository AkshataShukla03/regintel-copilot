1. Executive Summary

What it is, what it does, and why it matters.

RegIntel Copilot is an AI-powered compliance assistant built entirely on Snowflake that lets banking compliance teams ask natural language questions and receive governed, explainable, evidence-backed answers — covering the full workflow from fraud signal detection to audit-ready investigation reports.

The core differentiator is not fraud detection. There are thousands of fraud detection systems. The differentiator is: structured evidence + regulatory knowledge + governed AI + auditability — in a single conversational interface.

When an analyst asks "Why is this account high-risk, and what regulatory action is required?", the system simultaneously queries transaction data for evidence, searches policy documents for applicable rules, and produces a response that cites both — with a clear chain from signal to evidence to finding.

4.2 Fraud Detection

Two tabs: Structuring Detection + High-Risk Transactions

Structuring Detection: Shows 296 findings — repeated sub-threshold cash deposits within 48-hour aggregation windows, classified by severity (CRITICAL, HIGH, MEDIUM, LOW). Filterable by severity level.

High-Risk Transactions: 2,494 transactions scored across multiple risk signals. Filterable by fraud pattern type. Shows transaction ID, account, customer name, amount, risk score, and primary risk reason.

4.3 Alerts & Investigation

Alert Queue + Investigation Report Generator

Alert Queue: All 29 alerts filterable by status (Open, Under Investigation, Escalated, Closed). Joined with regulatory rules to show which regulation triggered each alert.

Investigation Report Generator: Select any alert → click "Generate Investigation Report" → the system produces a structured 7-section report:

  1. Subject Information — Customer name, ID, type, nationality, account details
  2. Risk Assessment — Composite score, PEP/sanctions flags, transaction counts
  3. Alert Details & Fraud Typology — Triggered rule, threshold, description
  4. Supporting Transaction Evidence — Individual transactions with amounts and dates
  5. Applicable Regulatory Requirements — Retrieved from policy docs by Cortex Agent
  6. Investigation Findings — Current status, SAR filing status, resolution notes
  7. Recommended Next Steps — Context-aware (PEP → senior approval, expired KYC → refresh, etc.)

Includes disclaimer: "This report is generated by an AI system and requires review by a qualified compliance analyst before any regulatory action is taken."

4.4 Customer Risk

Customer profiles with composite risk scoring

500 customers filterable by risk rating and minimum composite score. Shows PEP/sanctions flags, account count, balance, flagged transaction count, and active alerts. Includes KYC Renewal Required section for expired/failed/pending verifications.

4.5 Ask Copilot

Natural language interface backed by Cortex Agent

Chat interface powered by the REGINTEL_COPILOT_AGENT. The agent routes questions to the appropriate tool:

  • Data questions → Cortex Analyst queries the semantic view (governed SQL)
  • Policy questions → Cortex Search retrieves regulatory documents
  • Combined questions → Both tools used; response cites evidence + regulation

Example: "Does the structuring pattern on ACCT-0000001 meet our SAR investigation criteria? Cite the relevant policy." → Returns transaction evidence + BSA/SAR policy section + 30/60-day filing timeline.

5. Hero Workflow: Signal → Evidence → Finding

This is the core demo: the complete compliance workflow in one interaction.

The flow: Detection → Investigation → Regulatory Reasoning → Evidence → Documentation

Step 1: Detection

The Command Center shows $210.36M in modeled suspicious exposure across 2,477 flagged transactions. The Investigation Queue surfaces the highest-priority case: a CRITICAL structuring alert on account ACCT-0000001.

Step 2: Investigation

The analyst clicks into Alerts & Investigation → selects the alert → clicks "Generate Investigation Report." The system compiles: customer profile (KYC status, PEP flag, risk score), account details, the specific transactions that form the structuring pattern, and the regulatory rule that triggered the alert.

Step 3: Regulatory Reasoning

The analyst switches to Ask Copilot and asks: "Does this structuring pattern meet our SAR investigation criteria? Cite the relevant policy."

The Cortex Agent responds with a structured assessment:

  • Assessment: Potentially reportable — analyst review required
  • Transaction Evidence: 17 cash deposits, $164,700 aggregate, 48-hour window
  • Risk Indicators: Repeated sub-threshold deposits, aggregated cash activity, pattern inconsistent with baseline
  • Regulatory Context: BSA/SAR policy, Section 4.2
  • Filing Timeline: 30 calendar days from initial detection; up to 60 days if no suspect identified (per FinCEN)
  • Disclaimer: AI-generated analysis; final determination requires authorized compliance review

Step 4: Evidence & Documentation

The investigation report contains all evidence, regulatory context, and recommended next steps — ready for compliance review and case management filing.

Basel IIILiquidity Coverage Ratio, Capital Adequacy Ratios100% LCR minimum; 4.5% CET1, 8% total capital
OFACSDN list screening, blocking and reportingAll transactions screened; 10-day blocking report
FATFHigh-risk jurisdiction guidance, EDD recommendationsJurisdiction lists per current FATF publications
SAR Filing Nuance: The prototype models the $5,000 threshold applicable to banks under 31 CFR §1020.320. Thresholds vary by institution type and jurisdiction. The 30-day filing deadline includes the FinCEN extension: if no suspect is identified, filing may be delayed up to 60 calendar days total.

Production note: In production, regulatory scope would be scoped to the specific institution's obligations. FATF high-risk jurisdiction lists are time-sensitive and must be updated per current FATF publications. This prototype includes all frameworks to demonstrate cross-domain regulatory reasoning.

8. Limitations & Future Directions

Limitations

This is a production-oriented prototype built on synthetic data in a hackathon environment. A production deployment would require:

  • Real transaction data with proper access controls and encryption
  • Model validation and explainability testing
  • Regulatory change management processes
  • Disaster recovery and audit logging
  • Security review and penetration testing
  • Integration with existing case management and filing systems (e.g., FinCEN BSA E-Filing)
